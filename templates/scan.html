Tips for Identifying Phishing URLs

Check for Spelling Errors

Phishing URLs often contain misspellings or slight variations of legitimate domain names (e.g., "amaz0n.com" instead of "amazon.com").

Look for HTTPS

Legitimate websites typically use HTTPS encryption. While some phishing sites also use HTTPS, the absence of it is a red flag.

Beware of Shortened URLs

URL shorteners (like bit.ly or tinyurl.com) can hide the actual destination. Always hover over links to see where they lead.

Be Suspicious of Urgency

Phishing attempts often create a false sense of urgency to make you act quickly without thinking.
