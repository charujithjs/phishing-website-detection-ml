Tips for Identifying Phishing URLs

Check for Spelling Errors

Phishing URLs often contain misspellings or slight variations of legitimate domain names. For example, "amaz0n.com" instead of "amazon.com".

Look for HTTPS

Legitimate websites typically use HTTPS encryption. While phishing sites may also use HTTPS, the absence of it is a red flag.

Beware of Shortened URLs

URL shorteners like bit.ly or tinyurl.com can hide the actual destination. Always hover over links to see where they lead before clicking.

Watch for Excessive Subdomains

Phishing URLs may use multiple subdomains to make the URL look legitimate, such as "secure.paypal.account.com" where "account.com" is the actual domain.
